# Ad policy, from evidence

Ad platforms do not publish the rule you actually need. Enforcement varies by account, and a competitor running something is not proof you can run it. The only reliable signal is your own account's history, so keep one.

## Start the log

Create `ads/AD-POLICY.md` in your project. It has four parts.

**1. Rejection log.** Date, ad name, the exact stated reason, and the outcome. Reinstatement after appeal is not resolution, note it and keep watching.

**2. The evidence table.** One row per ad, columns for each attribute you suspect matters, and the status. This is the part that does the work:

| Ad | Money figures | Effort claim | Job-escape framing | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Fill a row for every ad, including the ones that are fine. **The approved ads are the evidence.** An attribute that appears in both approved and rejected ads is not your trigger.

**3. Conclusions, with confidence.** Write "high confidence" only where an attribute appears in the rejected ads and in none of the approved ones. Everything else is a hypothesis, label it as one.

**4. Mistakes made.** When a fix does not work, write down what you assumed and why it was wrong. This is the section that saves the most time later.

## Before writing any ad

- Read the log. Do not re-derive it.
- Check every number against your own verified figures. Never use a number you cannot screenshot.
- Check the destination page too, review looks at where the ad points.
- Avoid pairing an earnings figure with a claim about how little work it takes. That pairing is the single most commonly enforced combination in this category.

## After a rejection

1. Get the exact stated reason, not the category name.
2. Add the row and re-read the whole table. Ask what the rejected ad has that the approved ones do not.
3. If an ad has been rejected twice, stop editing it. Two rejections on one creative means the angle is the problem, not the execution.
4. Check whether the ad has ever earned anything before spending effort defending it.
